### Recommendations for Future Episodes

1. **Avoid chapter numbering in transcripts**: Let content flow naturally. Chapters work in books, but podcast conversations should feel organic.

2. **Watch for mechanism over-explanation**: When a discovery involves complex biochemistry, give listeners enough to understand why it matters without turning the podcast into a lecture. Section 05 originally spent 112 lines on COX-1/COX-2 when 30 lines sufficed.

3. **Identify the true pivot early**: In planning, distinguish between the discovery moment (Craven 1948) and the validation moment (Vane 1971). They're both important but serve different narrative functions.

4. **Soften dramatic claims that can't be fully verified**: "Some accounts suggest" is safer than "he stopped taking aspirin" when sources aren't definitive.

5. **Build better transitions**: Each section should end with a hook or question that makes the next section feel inevitable, not abrupt.

6. **Use background sources as fact-check anchors**: During editorial pass, verify that specific numbers, dates, and dramatic claims are supported by background research. Flag anything that seems unsupported.

7. **Trust the story's inherent drama**: The Craven tragedy is powerful even without embellishment. The 40-year delay is appalling without exaggeration. Let the facts carry the emotional weight.
