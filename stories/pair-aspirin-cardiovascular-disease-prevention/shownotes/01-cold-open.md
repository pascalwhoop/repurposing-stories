# Cold Open: The Tease

In 1897, a German chemist synthesized a white powder to soothe his father's arthritis—and created what would become the world's best-selling painkiller. But for 51 years, no one suspected this humble pill had a second, far more important superpower: preventing the heart attacks and strokes that kill more people than any other disease. Today, that discovery saves over a million lives annually—yet the man who figured it out died of a heart attack, ignored by the medical establishment, never knowing he was right.
