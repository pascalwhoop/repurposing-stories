# Dexamethasone for Severe COVID-19: A Drug Repurposing Story

## The Story in Brief

In March 2020, as the COVID-19 pandemic overwhelmed hospitals worldwide, Oxford University researchers launched the RECOVERY (Randomised Evaluation of COVID-19 Therapy) trial with unprecedented speed—the protocol was drafted, approved, and enrolling patients within nine days. Among the treatments tested was dexamethasone, a synthetic corticosteroid first synthesized in 1957 and approved by the FDA in 1958, originally developed by Merck to treat inflammatory and autoimmune conditions like rheumatoid arthritis and allergies.

On June 16, 2020, after enrolling over 11,000 patients in just 100 days, the RECOVERY trial announced breakthrough results: dexamethasone reduced mortality by one-third in ventilated COVID-19 patients and by one-fifth in those requiring supplemental oxygen. This was the first treatment proven to save lives in severe COVID-19. Within hours, dexamethasone became standard of care across the UK's National Health Service. Within weeks, it was adopted globally, transforming from a decades-old generic drug costing pennies per dose into one of medicine's most impactful pandemic interventions.

The discovery was not a serendipitous accident but a systematic evaluation of existing therapies during a crisis. Corticosteroids had shown mixed results in previous viral outbreaks, and early in the pandemic, the World Health Organization recommended against their use in COVID-19. The RECOVERY trial's rigorous design—a simple, pragmatic, open-label randomized trial embedded directly into routine clinical care—provided definitive evidence that changed global practice overnight.

## Why It Matters

Dexamethasone for severe COVID-19 represents a textbook case of successful drug repurposing under extraordinary circumstances. The story challenges the conventional wisdom that pandemics require novel therapeutics. Instead, an inexpensive, widely available, 62-year-old drug became a cornerstone of pandemic response, demonstrating that rigorous clinical trials of existing medications can deliver results faster than developing new ones.

The financial and human impact is staggering. By December 2020, dexamethasone was estimated to have saved approximately 12,000 lives in the UK alone and up to 650,000 lives globally. The treatment costs less than $1 per day—compared to remdesivir's $2,340-$3,120 price tag—making it accessible to low- and middle-income countries. The drug was already on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines since 1977, ensuring global availability.

The RECOVERY trial itself set new standards for pandemic research. By embedding clinical trials within routine hospital care, simplifying consent procedures, and using mortality as a straightforward primary endpoint, the trial recruited at a rate unmatched in medical research—over 500 patients per day at peak enrollment. This approach proved that large-scale, definitive trials can be conducted rapidly during public health emergencies without sacrificing scientific rigor.

## Key Facts

- **Original Use**: Approved October 30, 1958 by the FDA (NDA 011664, Merck) for inflammatory and autoimmune conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, allergic disorders, and adrenal insufficiency
- **Repurposed Use**: Emergency use for severe and critical COVID-19 (patients requiring oxygen or mechanical ventilation), adopted globally beginning June 2020
- **The Pivotal Discovery**: The RECOVERY trial, led by Professor Peter Horby and Professor Martin Landray at Oxford University, enrolled the first patient on March 19, 2020 and announced results on June 16, 2020—a 100-day turnaround that enrolled 11,303 participants
- **Impact Metrics**:
  - Reduced 28-day mortality by 36% in ventilated patients (rate ratio 0.65, 95% CI 0.48-0.88)
  - Reduced 28-day mortality by 20% in patients on oxygen (rate ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.67-0.96)
  - Estimated 12,000 lives saved in UK (July-December 2020) and 650,000 lives saved globally
  - Treatment cost: less than $1 per day; generic manufacturing cost as low as $0.19 per oral course
  - On WHO Model List of Essential Medicines since 1977
  - Adopted as standard of care globally within weeks of announcement

## The Lesson

Dexamethasone's repurposing for COVID-19 teaches us that pandemic preparedness requires not just vaccine development but systematic evaluation of existing drugs through rapid, pragmatic trials. The RECOVERY trial's success stemmed from institutional readiness (UK's centralized NHS), regulatory flexibility (approval in 8 days), and trial design simplicity (minimal data collection, mortality endpoint, embedded in clinical care).

The story also highlights a critical principle in drug repurposing: mechanism matters. Dexamethasone worked not by targeting the virus but by dampening the cytokine storm—the dysregulated immune response that kills severely ill COVID-19 patients. This explains why it helped only patients with advanced disease requiring oxygen support, and why earlier administration in mild cases showed no benefit and potential harm. The drug's 62 years of clinical experience meant its safety profile was well understood, accelerating adoption.

Perhaps most importantly, dexamethasone demonstrated that low-cost, off-patent drugs can be game-changers in global health crises. Its affordability and widespread availability meant that even resource-limited settings could implement evidence-based treatment immediately. The RECOVERY trial proved that rigorous science and rapid results are not mutually exclusive—a lesson that has reshaped how the medical community approaches pandemic therapeutics.
