# Chapter 3.5: Against the Consensus

ELENA: [thoughtful] Before we get to that June 8th meeting, we need to understand what the RECOVERY team was up against.

MARCUS: [serious] Because launching a trial of corticosteroids wasn't just bold—it was controversial.

ELENA: [emphatic] In March 2020, the WHO's guidance was explicit: "Routine corticosteroids should be avoided unless indicated for another reason." The message was clear—don't use steroids.

MARCUS: [analytical] Based on SARS and MERS data suggesting harm. Delayed viral clearance, increased secondary infections, possibly higher mortality.

ELENA: [serious] So when Horby and Landray decided to include dexamethasone in RECOVERY, they were challenging the global health consensus.

MARCUS: [thoughtful] That takes courage. Because if you're wrong—if dexamethasone causes harm—you're not just wrong about a drug. You're wrong about defying WHO guidance in the middle of a pandemic.

ELENA: [reflective] The headlines would write themselves: "Oxford scientists ignore WHO, waste resources on discredited treatment."

MARCUS: [impressed] But they had a mechanistic hypothesis that explained why the SARS and MERS data might not apply to COVID-19.

ELENA: [analytical] Right. SARS and MERS studies used high doses given early during viral replication. But COVID-19 deaths were happening later—during the hyperinflammatory phase—when a moderate steroid dose might help, not harm.

MARCUS: [emphatic] Timing and dosing matter. The mechanism predicted that dexamethasone should work in the right patients at the right time.

ELENA: [serious] But you can't prove that with a hypothesis. You need data. Rigorous, definitive data.

MARCUS: [excited] Which brings us to June 8, 2020. The day that data became clear enough to change the world.

ELENA: [intrigued] The pivot point.
