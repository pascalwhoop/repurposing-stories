# Cold Open: Sildenafil for Erectile Dysfunction

## The Hook (3 sentences)

When Pfizer scientists synthesized UK-92,480 in 1989 to treat chest pain, they were targeting a $2 billion cardiovascular market with rational drug design and solid mechanistic hypotheses. By 1993, after years of disappointing angina trials, the program was on the verge of termination—until a pattern emerged that no one had anticipated. Male trial participants were reporting a side effect they were reluctant to give up, and this "failure" would become the most famous drug repurposing story in pharmaceutical history, fundamentally changing how medicine and society approach sexual health.

## Opening Strategy Notes

**Purpose:** Establish the classic failure-to-success arc immediately; signal that this story involves both scientific elegance and cultural transformation.

**Tone:** Intrigue with a hint of inevitability—listeners know Viagra succeeded, but the cold open reframes it as a near-miss that required institutional courage to pursue.

**Stakes:** Commercial (termination vs. blockbuster), scientific (mechanism mismatch vs. perfect match), and societal (stigma vs. normalized discourse).

**Hook Elements:**
1. **Specific details anchor credibility:** UK-92,480, 1989, chest pain, $2B market
2. **Tension creation:** "on the verge of termination"
3. **Human element:** "reluctant to give up" hints at patient agency
4. **Scale signal:** "most famous drug repurposing story," "fundamentally changing"

**Transition to Episode:**
After cold open, hosts introduce themselves and the premise:
- Marcus: "Today we're talking about Viagra—but not the cultural phenomenon you think you know. We're digging into the science, the strategy, and the story of how a cardiovascular failure became a multi-billion dollar success."
- Elena: "And what I love about this story is that it's not actually an 'accident.' It's what happens when systematic observation meets mechanistic insight. Let's start at the beginning, in 1986 at Pfizer's research facility in Sandwich, Kent, England."
