# Dossier Overview: Thalidomide for Multiple Myeloma

## Executive Summary

This dossier documents the repurposing of thalidomide from a disgraced sedative withdrawn in 1961 after causing catastrophic birth defects to a breakthrough treatment for multiple myeloma approved by the FDA in 2006. This represents one of pharmaceutical history's most dramatic reversals: a drug whose name became synonymous with regulatory failure transformed into a life-extending cancer therapy generating tens of billions in revenue.

**Drug**: Thalidomide (α-N-phthalimidoglutarimide)
**Original Indication**: Sedative/hypnotic for insomnia and morning sickness (1956-1961)
**Repurposed Indication**: Multiple myeloma (FDA approved 2006); erythema nodosum leprosum (FDA approved 1998)
**Mechanism Shift**: From GABAergic sedation to angiogenesis inhibition and immunomodulation via cereblon binding

## Timeline of Key Events

- **1953-1954**: Thalidomide synthesized independently by CIBA (Switzerland, 1953) and Chemie Grünenthal (Germany, 1954)[^1]
- **1956-1961**: Marketed in 46 countries as sedative; caused ~10,000 birth defects worldwide; withdrawn 1961[^2]
- **1964**: First report of thalidomide's anti-inflammatory properties in erythema nodosum leprosum[^3]
- **1965**: Israeli physician Jacob Sheskin discovers thalidomide efficacy for leprosy symptoms[^4]
- **1994**: Robert D'Amato and Judah Folkman publish evidence that thalidomide inhibits angiogenesis[^5]
- **1997-1998**: Bart Barlogie at University of Arkansas conducts first compassionate-use trial in multiple myeloma[^6]
- **July 16, 1998**: FDA approves thalidomide (Thalomid) for erythema nodosum leprosum with unprecedented STEPS safety program[^7]
- **November 1999**: Nikhil Singhal publishes NEJM paper showing 32% response rate in refractory myeloma (n=84)[^8]
- **May 26, 2006**: FDA grants accelerated approval for thalidomide + dexamethasone in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma[^9]
- **2010**: Hiroshi Handa discovers cereblon (CRBN) as thalidomide's molecular target[^10]
- **2013**: Mechanism elucidated: thalidomide-cereblon complex degrades IKZF1/3 transcription factors[^11]

## Disease Context: Multiple Myeloma in the 1990s

Multiple myeloma is a malignant proliferation of plasma cells in bone marrow, representing 1% of all cancers and 10% of hematologic malignancies. In the 1990s:

- **Incidence**: ~4-5 cases per 100,000 persons annually; ~14,000 new U.S. diagnoses yearly[^12]
- **Median age at diagnosis**: 66-70 years
- **Standard treatment**: Melphalan + prednisone (introduced 1960s), with VAD (vincristine-doxorubicin-dexamethasone) for refractory disease
- **Median survival**: 29-37 months with melphalan-prednisone[^13]
- **Five-year survival**: 28.8% (1990-1992)[^14]
- **Clinical challenge**: No new therapies in 30+ years; uniformly fatal disease

## The Discovery Arc

The thalidomide-myeloma connection emerged from converging scientific insights:

1. **Angiogenesis hypothesis** (1971-1994): Judah Folkman proposed tumors require blood vessel growth; in 1994 his team showed thalidomide blocks angiogenesis[^15]
2. **Myeloma angiogenesis** (1996): Studies showed increased bone marrow vascularity in myeloma patients correlated with disease progression[^16]
3. **Clinical intuition** (1997): Bart Barlogie, aware of Folkman's work and facing desperate myeloma patients, initiated compassionate-use trial
4. **Breakthrough trial** (1997-1998): 84 patients with refractory myeloma treated; 32% response rate, including complete remissions[^17]
5. **Publication** (1999): NEJM paper by Singhal et al. catalyzed worldwide adoption

## Regulatory and Commercial Trajectory

**1998 FDA Approval (Leprosy)**:
- Indication: Erythema nodosum leprosum (ENL)
- Celgene secured exclusive U.S. distribution rights
- Mandatory STEPS (System for Thalidomide Education and Prescribing Safety) program:
  - Physician/pharmacist/patient registration required
  - Mandatory contraception and pregnancy testing for women of childbearing potential
  - Weekly pregnancy tests first 4 weeks, then monthly[^18]

**2006 FDA Approval (Multiple Myeloma)**:
- Indication: Newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (with dexamethasone)
- Based on Phase III trial showing 63% response rate vs. 46% for dexamethasone alone
- Time to progression: 22.6 months (thalidomide+dex) vs. 6.5 months (dex alone)[^19]
- Accelerated approval pathway
- Orphan drug designation (7-year market exclusivity)[^20]

**Commercial Evolution**:
- Celgene revenue: $26.2 million (1999) → $12.9 billion (2017)[^21]
- Thalidomide derivatives developed:
  - Lenalidomide (Revlimid): FDA approved 2005, generated $8.1 billion in 2017
  - Pomalidomide (Pomalyst): FDA approved 2013
- Combined IMiDs revenue: $6.97 billion (2015), 75% of Celgene total[^22]
- Bristol Myers Squibb acquired Celgene for $74 billion (2019)[^23]

## Scientific Impact

**Mechanism Understanding**:
- Initial hypothesis: Anti-angiogenic via FGF-2 and VEGF inhibition[^24]
- TNF-α suppression identified as anti-inflammatory mechanism[^25]
- **2010 breakthrough**: Cereblon (CRBN) identified as direct molecular target[^26]
- **2013 mechanism**: Thalidomide binds cereblon E3 ubiquitin ligase, inducing degradation of IKZF1 and IKZF3 transcription factors essential for myeloma cell survival[^27]

**Clinical Outcomes Impact**:
- Median myeloma survival increased from ~30 months (1990s) to ~50 months (2007)[^28]
- Five-year survival: 28.8% (1990-1992) → 46% (2010)[^29]
- Most substantial improvements occurred 2001-2007 (45% increase in median survival)[^30]

**Drug Development Legacy**:
- Validated angiogenesis inhibition as cancer therapy strategy
- Created IMiD drug class with superior analogs
- Established modern REMS framework for high-risk drugs
- Demonstrated viability of repurposing disgraced/withdrawn drugs

## Ethical and Policy Dimensions

**Safety Infrastructure**:
- STEPS program (1998-2004): 124,000 patients registered (43% female)[^31]
- Effectiveness: Only 1 confirmed pregnancy in women taking thalidomide under program[^32]
- Evolution: STEPS transitioned to THALOMID REMS program
- Global challenge: Thalidomide used off-label in countries without REMS, causing new birth defects in Brazil and elsewhere[^33]

**Patent Controversy**:
- Thalidomide itself was off-patent, but Celgene patented:
  - REMS distribution system (14 patents)[^34]
  - New formulations and combinations
  - Derivatives (lenalidomide, pomalidomide)
- Strategy blocked generic competition through 2010s despite lack of drug patent[^35]
- Criticism: Used safety program as commercial barrier rather than purely protective measure[^36]

**Access and Equity**:
- Revlimid pricing: Increased from ~$60,000/year (2005) to $250,000+/year (2017)[^37]
- Generic thalidomide available but infrequently used in U.S.
- International disparities: Licensed for myeloma in Australia, New Zealand, Turkey, Israel, South Korea, but access varies[^38]
